What my first year of CS is teaching me

I'm seven weeks into a Computer Science and AI degree at the University of Nottingham. These are the lessons that have stuck so far. I'm writing them down so that in a few years I can check which ones survived.

01C shows you what Python hides

Python let me build things quickly. C made me understand them. Memory, pointers, what a list actually costs: none of it is visible until a language stops doing it for you.

Learning both at once feels like learning to drive and learning how the engine works in the same week. It's slower. It's also the only way I've found to stop treating my tools as magic.

02Math is the real language of AI

Every model I read about eventually comes down to linear algebra, probability and calculus. The code is usually the easy part. The math is the part that tells you why something works, and when it won't.

I used to see math lectures as the thing standing between me and the "real" computer science. Now they feel like the source code.

Being confused isn't a sign you're in the wrong place. It's usually a sign you're in the right one.

03Start before you feel ready

CS50 taught me this before university did. The first problem sets felt impossible. Then, a few weeks later, they didn't. Nothing changed except that I kept going.

Most of what I know came from starting something slightly too hard, getting stuck, and searching my way out. YouTube lectures, documentation, and a lot of failed attempts did more than any single course.

04Tools are leverage

Learning Linux and Git felt like a detour from programming. It wasn't. An hour spent getting comfortable in a terminal pays back a little every day after. Good tools don't make you smarter, but they remove the friction between an idea and trying it.

05Learn in public

This site is part of that. Writing forces me to find out what I actually understand, and what I only think I understand. Publishing it makes me finish.

If you're also early in this, I'd like to compare notes.
